# Research charter

Established 19 October 2026 from the user's direction.

The enduring objective is to develop concepts and methods that make a solid, defensible addition to the field. Publication is a means of communicating that contribution; obtaining a particular astronomical interpretation is not the success criterion.

The intended sequence is a substantive Nabta Playa case study, followed by a larger, independently informative study that tests whether the approach can become a repeatable framework. The first paper must stand on its own. The second must test transferability rather than merely repeat the first analysis on more data.

Working commitments proposed by the assessment:

- Start with an archaeological question, explicit feature definitions, and traceable evidence.
- Distinguish source discrepancies, measurement uncertainty, analytical choices, astronomical compatibility, and cultural interpretation.
- Permit positive, negative, and unresolved findings. State when the evidence cannot discriminate between hypotheses.
- Compare the contribution with existing methods and software. Reproducibility alone does not establish novelty.
- Keep exploratory discoveries distinct from independently specified tests; evaluate the complete selection process when making statistical claims.
- Preserve inherited materials in `legacy/`. Develop future accepted analyses separately, with documented decisions and reproducible outputs.
- Make every manuscript claim proportionate to its evidence and limitations.

The user's objective and two-study intention are established. Detailed methods, scope, venue, and second-case selection remain research decisions. See [the assessment](evidence/ASSESSMENT.md) for recommendations and readiness criteria.
