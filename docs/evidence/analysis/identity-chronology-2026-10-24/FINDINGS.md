# Identity and chronology evidence review — 24 October 2026

## Result and scope

The acquired 2025 monograph removes the source-access obstacle but does not supply a verified point-by-point bridge between the historical coordinate tables and later mapped remains. It exposes a specific conflict in the A identities, in addition to the previously identified distinction between map fragments and representative survey points. A reconstruction claiming original ancient bearings must not merge these records until the conflict and point definitions are resolved. A documentary case study can still report these limits and the validated historical-table analyses.

This review does not claim a new stellar solution, a construction date, statistical significance or demonstrated novelty. All derived outputs remain under agent_artifacts; legacy material is unchanged.

## A-4 / A-X conflict

Sources inspected:

- Wendorf and Malville 2001, printed p. 490, Fig. 16.1 (local PDF p. 501), clearly labels A-X near A-5, south of A-4. Printed p. 491 (PDF p. 502) describes A-X as a separate slab approximately 50 m west of A-5. The early map and description therefore do not support locating A-X with A-4.
- The original table on p. 498 and its later repeated/satellite/GPS versions are transcribed in the existing coordinate ledger. The published positions give A-4–A-X distances of 160.97 m (original/repeated table), 165.83 m (QuickBird), and 171.17 m (GPS), under assumed WGS84.
- Schild 2025, chapter 7, printed p. 140 (PDF p. 142), gives Sector B square r/20 for both A-X and A-4. It places A5 and A6 together in x/20. The relevant map is Fig. 2.3, printed p. 27.

The duplicated r/20 location is not reconciled by coordinate rounding. With each printed latitude permitted to vary by 0.00005 degrees under a nearest-rounding scenario, the minimum north–south separation of A-4 and A-X is still approximately 132.88 m. A square with side at most 50 m has a diagonal at most 70.71 m. The conditional same-square interpretation therefore fails even with this deliberately generous map-square size. A separate native-raster scale check uses 34 grid intervals spanning approximately x=20–962 and the 200 m scale bar spanning x=699–896 in the 974-pixel-wide image. Allowing each picked endpoint ±2 pixels implies a cell side of roughly 27.45–28.83 m. This is a drawing-scale check, not survey accuracy; the 50 m scenario is deliberately more permissive and does not depend on reference north.

This test is conditional on matching labels, comparable physical positions and the stated datum/rounding model. It identifies mutually incompatible uses of the published records; it does not identify which publication is wrong or rule out relabelling, changed observation points, gross coordinate errors or displacement. A common rigid registration or north rotation cannot remove a pairwise-distance conflict. A-X must not be silently moved to fit the later square or the older alignment.

The A5/A6 same-square pairing does not fail the deliberately permissive test. Passing this screen is not proof of identity. The repeated original table is dependent evidence, not another survey.

The old description's approximate westward offset of A-X from A5 is not reproduced exactly by all numerical versions either: the original table puts it about 34.78 m away at bearing 342.79 degrees, whereas QB/GPS put it about 32.79 m away at 289.74 degrees. This is further reason to preserve source versions rather than treating narrative, map and table as interchangeable measurements.

## B1/B2 representation limit

The completed map audit extracts 28 red drawing paths in ten cells identified in chapter 7 as B1/B2. The text's fragment counts disagree with the path counts in n/19, o/25, o/26 and o/27. The catalogue supports the locations and clustered/fragmented character, but does not provide the old individual coordinate-table IDs on these drawings. This is a representation mismatch, not a finding of missing stones. The map audit records the exact cells, pages, paths and qualification.

The required observational unit is still unresolved: original stele base, one fragment, a cluster centre, or a representative survey point. More precise digitization cannot answer that archaeological question.

## Date labels and conditional chronology

Five matched target-date ranges in Malville et al. 2007 p. 5 and Malville 2015 p. 1086 have an exact 2000-year arithmetic offset between labels BC and BP: A3/Arcturus, A2/Arcturus, A1/Arcturus, B1/Sirius and B1/alpha Centauri. Their original labels are retained. We do not silently normalize these as radiocarbon BP or infer an epoch definition not established in the checked passages. Orion is not forced into this exact matching exercise: the 2015 entry names the belt and has a different range.

The 2025 quarry discussion, printed pp. 104–105, repeats the older BC ranges and proposes activity around 4600–4200/4100 BC. It explicitly allows undated quarry pits and potentially later extraction. It also notes unsuccessful attempts to obtain direct dates for particular installations. The sixteen charcoal determinations must not become sixteen independent row-construction dates.

`chronology_compatibility.csv` compares the seven dated 2007 target claims against both proposed contextual windows, keeping all quantities in their source-labelled BC convention. This is interval arithmetic, not astronomical recomputation or radiocarbon recalibration.

Intersection duration is not probability or evidential weight. A3 and both B1 candidates remain compatible with either window. A2 depends appreciably on the contextual endpoint. A1 and the later B2/Sirius interpretation conflict with a hard reading of either window; the sources do not establish that hard restriction for every row. Therefore the admissible conclusion is conditional tension, not rejection. B1 chronology alone does not select between its two proposed stellar targets. Reusing a star-fit date as independent support for that same identification would be circular.

## Consequence for the intended paper

The plausible case-study contribution is to show which conclusions survive the transition from a published number, to an identified physical feature, to an astronomical interpretation. Concrete examples now include:

1. A numerical reproduction can succeed while the identity and point-definition bridge remains unresolved.
2. Stable relative ordering in the historical coordinate scenarios does not certify accurate absolute ancient orientations.
3. A contextual chronology can constrain an interpretation conditionally without identifying a target or dating a row directly.

These are candidate contributions, not proof of novelty. Brophy and Rosen 2005 already reassess the coordinate evidence. Silva 2020, sections 2–3, already develops uncertainty distributions and coordinate transformations; its starting point is an identified architectural feature. Our unresolved identity alternatives cannot simply be disguised as repeated measurement noise or pooled with arbitrary weights. The useful comparison is whether a separate identity-admissibility stage changes the Nabta inference; the general methodological claim awaits validation beyond this case.

A 24 October targeted search for Haynie 2014 and its DOI did not yield a usable primary full text; the DOI browser request failed. Secondary search results were not used to infer its methods. That specific comparator remains unread. No systematic or exhaustive novelty claim is warranted.

## Evidence needed before reconstructed ancient target testing

Priority 1: an authoritative correction or survey/field record linking A-X, A-4 and A5 to the 2025 grid and the earlier coordinates. At minimum it must explain the r/20 duplication. A source specifying original observation points and dates is more useful than a higher-resolution copy of the same map.

Priority 2: the labelled coordinate or survey-point inventory behind B1/B2 and Complex Structure A. It should distinguish original bases, fragments, cluster representatives and the chosen observation origin. An explicit mapping is needed, not automatic nearest-neighbour matching.

Priority 3: the unrounded measurements and reduction used for the final combined directions, including weights, corrections, datum and north reference. This is needed to reproduce that final reduction, not to reproduce the already validated recalculated-WM column.

For direct construction dates, additional archaeological association evidence would be required. A different calibration alone cannot establish the association. For first-visibility target tests, horizon and atmospheric conventions must be explicit and tested, and modern catalogue data and long-term propagation independently validated.

These are branch-specific evidence requirements. Without them we can publish only conclusions proportionate to the documentary record, not a claimed recovery of true prehistoric orientations or a unique target/date. It remains scientifically possible to write a source-critical case study, but publication readiness and novelty are not yet established.
