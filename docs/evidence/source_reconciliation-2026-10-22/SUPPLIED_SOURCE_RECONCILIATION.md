# Supplied-source reconciliation — 23 October 2026

This advances the later-source case study following the decision to proceed without Sahara 5:7–16. It is a documentary reconciliation, not a new astronomical result. Original materials remain unchanged.

## Nature 1998: preserve the original hypothesis set

Malville et al., Nature 392:488–491, DOI 10.1038/33131, p.489 reports three northern directions (24.3°, 25°, 28°) plus 90.02° and 126°. It describes the 1997 mapping as combining theodolite and differential GPS. These are five reported directions, whereas the later tables divide the remains into six groups. No one-to-one crosswalk is established here. In particular, the due-east feature must not silently become B1 or B2.

The printed origin is 22°30′29.7″N, 30°43′31.2″W. The W hemisphere is visibly printed and inconsistent with the site's Egyptian location. Preserve this transcription and flag it; any computational use of an east-longitude interpretation must be an explicit assumption. Even with that assumption, this is not the same coordinate as the later rounded table origin (22.5080, 30.7256). A mixed-source origin/stone calculation would be a sensitivity scenario, not a recovered survey.

The original 10° east-of-north description is attributed to the missing Sahara article. Its historical provenance remains indirect; the later numerical data do not depend on treating that early line as a verified reconstruction.

## Iwaniszewski: a separate calendar-circle question

The recovered chapter is published in 2021 proceedings of the 2009 conference, printed pp.10–13 / PDF pp.19–22. It is no longer an inaccessible critique. Its principal subject is E-92-9, not a replacement survey of the radial rows.

At p.12, the contrast is 62° in Nature versus a 65–70° viewing direction in Applegate and Zedeño. These are not automatically two measurements of the same precisely defined axis. The latter is a broad viewing aperture; 5° must not become a Gaussian measurement error. Figure 2 and Table 1 were checked visually against the supplied PDF.

Table 1 reports sunrise azimuths for 4890 BCE: zenith-passage first/centre/last contacts 65°06′ / 65°13′ / 65°20′; solstitial contacts 63°08′ / 63°15′ / 63°22′. These are model outputs under the chapter's horizon, refraction, elevation and obliquity assumptions, not observed stone bearings. The zenith-passage proposal is a competing interpretation, not demonstrated intent.

The chapter uses a 6000±60 conventional-BP hearth date to suggest construction shortly after 4890 BCE. The sourcebook's chapter 14 is more cautious: construction is not strictly radiometrically defined, and stones were placed after at least some nearby hearths were abandoned. Relative sequencing alone does not establish construction within a few decades, nor prove a secure relationship to the specifically dated hearth. Preserve the measurement, calibrated interpretation and construction inference separately.

The Sirius visibility argument also extrapolates an earlier table across time and latitude (p.12, footnote 4). It would need independent calculation before use. It is outside the present radial geometry analysis.

## Scope and next analytical requirements

The core case study remains: how much do source lineage, feature membership, observation-point definition and published spatial precision affect the reproducibility and interpretability of the radial-alignment claims?

1. Reproduce each source's calculation using its own coordinate observations and named origin. Preserve sourcebook versus repeated-source differences.
2. Report membership alternatives separately, including incomplete alternatives; do not supply missing A-10 coordinates or silently equate C-6/C-8.
3. Quantify differences between source scenarios before introducing probability distributions. Coordinate decimal resolution is not survey accuracy; shared origins and repeat measurements create dependencies.
4. Keep the five Nature directions as their own historical claim set until physical identities are demonstrated. Do not use nearest-angle matching as proof of identity.
5. Require an archaeological consequence beyond already-published disagreements before claiming novelty. A reproducibility result alone does not validate intentional astronomy or a transferable framework.
6. Keep the calendar-circle analysis optional and separate. Its aperture definitions and chronology require a distinct model; adding it prematurely would broaden the paper without resolving the radial question.

Sahara remains a gap in discovery history, not a blanket analysis gate. Other missing evidence—unrounded survey records, label crosswalks and reduction details—may restrict conclusions, but conditional analysis can quantify those restrictions now.
