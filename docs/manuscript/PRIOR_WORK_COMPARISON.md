## Positioning decision

The present contribution is best framed as an evidence-limited archaeological case study with reproducible quantitative diagnostics. It does not yet justify a new general statistical framework. The positive continuous-domain ordering result and the unresolved physical-identity bridge are complementary findings: rejecting an unjustified reconstruction does not require discarding all structure in the published coordinates.

The practical consequence is a more specific evidence requirement. Digitization resolution, a more accurate stellar model, or a narrower assumed instrument error cannot decide whether A-X and A-4 have been correctly mapped to the later grid. A documented identity correction and pointwise survey linkage would address that issue. Conversely, the demonstrated ordering can be reported without pretending to possess those missing records.

## Corrections carried into the draft

- Say that exact feature-to-point correspondence is unresolved, not that no observation-point convention was published. BR2005 provides an estimated-centre convention; its physical implementation is not uniquely recoverable from the checked material.
- Do not present repeated WM coordinates as an independent survey.
- Do not describe continuous spherical enclosures as certified WGS84 extrema or survey-error confidence intervals.
- Do not equate contextual chronological compatibility with support for a unique target, or non-overlap with a direct archaeological refutation.
- Do not claim a comprehensive novelty search or first methodological discovery.
