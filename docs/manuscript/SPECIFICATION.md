## Continuous rounding analysis

The new calculation uses spherical initial bearings and the historical arithmetic mean of individual origin-to-point bearings. Each printed latitude and longitude is allowed to vary continuously by ±0.00005 degrees. This is a nearest-rounding scenario, not an empirical error model or probability distribution.

The origin is shared between the groups in each contrast. Its two-dimensional interval is partitioned into 1, 16 and 256 boxes. Each stone retains its whole coordinate box within each origin subdivision. Interval arithmetic encloses the trigonometric bearing calculation and the difference between group means; combining the enclosures covers the full declared domain. The implementation checks that numerator and denominator remain positive throughout, avoiding branch wrapping and singularities in this northern/eastern geometry. Subdivision tightens bounds but does not omit intermediate origin values.

For 256 origin boxes, all twelve source-version/contrast combinations have positive lower enclosures. The smallest adjacent lower enclosures are approximately 0.561876 degrees for A2 minus A3 and 0.960689 degrees for A1 minus A2. These are conservative enclosure endpoints, not exact attained minima or confidence limits. The duplicated WM version is not an independent survey.

Checks include refinement containment, nominal spherical-formula agreement with pyproj within 3.9e-11 degrees, and 24,000 independently calculated sampled contrasts lying inside the enclosures. Sampling is an implementation cross-check, not the justification for coverage of the continuous domain. The calculation uses mpmath interval arithmetic at 35 decimal digits; it is not a formal proof-assistant certificate. The earlier WGS84 grid result remains separately labelled and is not upgraded by this spherical calculation.

## Prior-work comparison and publication test

Brophy and Rosen 2005 already perform coordinate reassessment. Silva 2020 already models orientation uncertainty and celestial transformations. Thus numerical reassessment, distributions, and transformations cannot be claimed as new concepts here.

The candidate contribution is a demonstrated separation between properties that are identifiable from printed coordinates and properties requiring an independently established physical observation unit. The A ordering supplies a positive result; the A-X and B mapping problems explain why that result cannot automatically be promoted to an ancient astronomical conclusion. The chronology comparison similarly distinguishes conditional consistency from target discrimination.

Before a submission-ready manuscript, the comparison must establish whether this evidence-gating treatment adds a new Nabta-specific finding or a useful methodological application beyond existing work. The user supplied Haynie 2014; its full text was reviewed on 25 October. It establishes direct prior work on coordinate-based reassessment, visibility assumptions, alternative targets and contextual dating. The narrower source-lineage, continuous-domain and 2025-map findings require proportionate positioning; see the source review. No strong absence-of-prior-work claim is permitted.
