## Assessment of our progress

A working draft and repeatable calculations are useful research artifacts, but do not establish that we chose the strongest question or performed the most informative analyses. The previous next-step list overemphasized citation checking and preparation. A scientific review must precede that stage. Several questions were already raised in ASSESSMENT.md and were not explicitly closed when the project narrowed to a documentary case study.

The archaeological case remains plausible: source lineage and physical observation units affect what can be inferred from published bearings. The present evidence does not yet show that our particular combination of diagnostics contributes enough beyond a careful replication and source critique. That is an open test, not an assertion that the paper fails.

## Questions to reopen

1. **What does the reader learn that changes an inference?** A-X/A-4 incompatibility could change whether datasets can be merged. Northern ordering could preserve a limited property despite poor absolute reconstruction. We must demonstrate the consequence of each; a stable ordering of already separated groups may be expected and insufficient on its own.
2. **Did the available outputs choose the question for us?** The positive northern result emerged during exploration. Record that provenance. It is a conditional descriptive result, not a prospectively selected confirmation or a chance-alignment test. Report the eligibility and outcomes of other group contrasts rather than selecting successes silently.
3. **What uncertainties interact?** Source version, membership, coordinate recording convention, origin, point definition and estimand are currently handled mostly as separate diagnostics. A claim that survives one axis need not survive a defensible combination. Not all combinations are coherent: prohibit mixing a coordinate version with undocumented identities, or combining mutually exclusive memberships.
4. **Which missing information is essential to which question?** Missing prehistoric positions blocks reconstruction, but not the printed-table question. Missing private coordinates behind Haynie's Figure 2 blocks reproduction of that figure's fits, not our whole paper. An unavailable input must not become a blanket stop or an invented value.
5. **What competing accounts survive?** Independent radial sightlines, a constructed row axis, preservation/fragment-centre artifacts, documentary relabelling and terrestrial organization need different evidence. Distinguish alternatives that can be assessed with the available record from ones that can only be acknowledged. A precise fit does not select among them.

## Methodological options and prior work

A disciplined analysis across admissible alternatives is preferable to a collection of isolated sensitivity plots. The general idea is established: Steegen et al. (2016) describe analysis across defensible data-processing choices, explicitly excluding inconsistent combinations. We can adapt that approach to the source/membership problem without claiming a new general method. [Primary article](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691616658637).

Simulation is also established in archaeoastronomy. Silva (2017) evaluates accuracy and precision for curvigram and maximum-likelihood approaches under a single-target scenario. Any simulation addition here must address the distinct documentary/feature-identification question, not claim novelty from using simulation. [Publisher abstract](https://journal.equinoxpub.com/JSA/article/view/10740).

Silva (2020) supplies probabilistic orientation modelling and a significance test. We should assess applicability and assumptions, not presume our bounded scenarios supersede it. Numerical intervals without empirical probability distributions answer a different question. [Author-institution full text](https://eprints.bournemouth.ac.uk/33905/7/1-s2.0-S0305440320300601-main.pdf).

Haynie (2015), following the now-read 2014 paper, develops a unified-design/comet interpretation. Its existence means the comparator inventory must extend beyond the formerly missing article when discussing alternative site-wide explanations. This review screened the publisher abstract only; no assessment of its full celestial calculations is claimed. [Publisher PDF](https://www.maajournal.com/index.php/maa/article/download/185/139/262).

Formal links to partial identification or imprecise-probability theory are candidates for a focused methods search, not concepts we have yet established as the right framing. Adding terminology without a practical gain would weaken the paper.

## Strongest objections a reviewer could make

- The stable order is already apparent in source-defined groups and has limited archaeological significance.
- The paper selects a favourable northern subset after seeing the results.
- Nearest-rounding assumptions yield precise mathematical bounds around poorly established observation units.
- The combined-survey failure test is too restricted to explain the historical discrepancy.
- A-X/A-4 is a naming or transcription issue, so the manuscript overstates it as a physical finding.
- Context-window intersections add arithmetic but little new chronological inference.
- The source ledger is valuable scholarship but does not substantiate a new framework.

Each objection needs either a discriminating test, a source-supported answer, or a narrower claim. None is answered simply by rerunning the software. These objections are review hypotheses, not established defects in the source authors' work.

## What should not be added merely to enlarge the project

No machine-learning layer, indiscriminate sky scan, unsupported probability distributions, unconstrained georeferencing, assumption-heavy radiocarbon construction model, or general framework claim based on one case. Equally, lack of raw data is not a reason to abandon all informative bounds or source-critical tests. Add a method only if it can change a conclusion, distinguish an alternative, expose a failure mode, or specify a useful evidence requirement.
